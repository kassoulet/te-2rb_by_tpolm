TE-2RB by TPOLM

A black-and-white 640x480 demo (The Gathering 1998 party version; the INFOFILE says it "is _supposed_ to be black & white 640x480"). Typographic slides, credits, and small 3D scenes (a TV, a wire lattice, a pulsating sphere, a bomb-like mesh, a wireframe finale) over a tracker module. Play it.

Status: complete. All 3005 frames of the picture (scenes 0 to 12, music 0:00 to 5:00) match the original exactly, pixel for pixel (3005/3005). The last 67 frames of music (5:00 to 5:07) have no picture in the original; the player keeps the last, nearly black frame.

How the original works

  • One 640x480 byte buffer. Every layer (noise grain, slide, sprite, 3D) is added to it modulo 256; a 256-entry grey palette is written to the VGA DAC every frame, so fades, flashes and pulsing are palette changes.
  • The grain comes from two 1.2 MB noise buffers built at start-up from the C rand() LCG; each frame copies a window of one of them at a sine-chosen offset.
  • The 3D is a tiny additive renderer: filled triangles are drawn as edge deltas (+I on the left edge, -I on the right) and one prefix-sum pass rebuilds the interiors; no z-buffer, no sorting. Shading is flat and linear in the angle (an acos).
  • The cameras are the 3DS keyframer tracks (resampled with a Hermite spline whose TCB values the loader discards); the wire lattice scene uses a second, separate 3D engine with Bezier lines.
  • The 3D has two render modes besides the filled one: wire (mode 2) and mode 8, which is the filled mode plus an outline added straight into the screen. The trail scenes (4, 6, 9 to 12) average each frame with the previous one, so a frame depends on its predecessor.
  • Scenes wait on the music position (order and row compared as one integer) and read a 70 Hz timer; nothing else drives them.

How this port works

DEMO.EXE (PMODE/W, Watcom 32-bit) was unpacked by running its own stub under the Unicorn emulator, then run with emulated DOS/DPMI/VESA/VGA and a virtual clock to capture ground-truth frames (640x480 index buffer plus palette). The JavaScript port is then diffed against those frames pixel by pixel. Frames 0 to 3004 have 0 differing pixels. Because every frame is a function of the music position, the player can pause and seek anywhere (the trail scenes replay from their start on a seek: about 1 s at worst in node, 1.7 s at the very end, not measured in a browser). The picture is 640x480 shown at 4:3.

The ending (scenes 9 to 12)

Scenes 9 and 10 are the bomb mesh again, now as a trail over the slides nowhere and nowh2, with the light on the camera eye; scene 10 uses render mode 8 (fill plus outline), the camera slowly pulls away and the palette runs through negative fade values. Scenes 11 and 12 draw the TPOLM1 mesh as wire over the creds and copyrigh slides and fade out to the end.

Honest limits: the picture is drawn on the capture's 10 frames-per-second grid; sin/cos are JS doubles, not the x87, and the 3D code keeps x87 intermediates in doubles (no difference seen on any of the 3005 checked frames); the camera and paths are verified only on the emulator's virtual-clock run; a few start-up values (the random generator state, some clock origins) are measured from the emulator rather than derived; the music is played by libopenmpt and was not compared sample by sample; three unused scene functions and the pre-demo slide are not ported. A WebGL rework is planned for later; this faithful 640x480 port stays the reference.
